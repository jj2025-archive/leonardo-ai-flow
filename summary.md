This is an insight into the mind of the author:

Someone fascinated by retro-futuristic vibes where old technology never died... it just got weirder, more tactile, and mysteriously beautiful. They crave a digital world that *feels* like something you can touch: sleek metal, glowing screens, analog buttons.

They long for anonymity in a surveilled world, treating ancient code and genetic blueprints as treasures to excavate and repurpose. Their imagination builds dimly-lit laboratories where biology and technology merge, interfaces that look like glossy 2005 dashboards controlling life itself.

Yet beneath the neon glow and cyberpunk aesthetics lies a quiet rebellion, a rejection of seamless, frictionless tech in favor of something raw, inefficient, and defiantly human. 

For them, the future isn't clean or invisible... it's loud, metallic, deeply *human*, and nostalgic for a past that never quite existed, even as they question whether we should have trusted technology in the first place.
